# Audio Platform IP and Trade Mark Pipeline

**Status:** Current working standard  
**Scope:** Story-name screening, originality review, ownership, licensing and trade mark decisions  
**Owner:** Audio Platform  
**Last updated:** 29 Jul 2026

This runbook protects creative effort before the Audio Platform Draft Pipeline commits substantial time to scripts, artwork, audio or public branding. It is a practical risk-management process, not legal advice and not a guarantee that no third-party claim will arise.

The intended sequence is:

```text
Story idea
  → IP and Trade Mark Pipeline
  → Audio Platform Draft Pipeline
  → Audio Platform Review Pipeline
  → Audio Platform Public Pipeline
```

## 1. Purpose

The pipeline must:

- identify obvious naming and intellectual-property risks early;
- distinguish a title problem from a story problem;
- preserve worthwhile concepts through renaming, differentiation and reworking;
- prevent expensive production before manageable risks are understood;
- record ownership and licence evidence for scripts, images, audio and other assets;
- determine when trade mark expenditure is commercially justified;
- create an auditable decision record for every story.

The pipeline must not reject a promising story merely because its first title or first expression needs work.

## 2. Core principle

A failed title does not automatically mean a failed story.

Use this order of preference:

1. Keep the story and title where the risk is acceptable.
2. Keep the story but rename it where the title is the main problem.
3. Keep the central idea but differentiate the characters, world, mechanism or execution.
4. Extract independently created and reusable elements from a concept that cannot proceed as written.
5. Abandon the project only when its essential value depends on material that cannot safely be used.

Copyright and trade marks address different risks:

- copyright protects original expression recorded in scripts, artwork, recordings and related materials;
- a trade mark protects a sign used to distinguish goods or services, such as a story-series name or platform brand;
- a general idea, genre, theme or archetype is not treated as owned merely because another work uses it;
- a trade mark application does not grant ownership of the underlying story concept.

## 3. Effort-control principle

The time limits in this runbook are decision guides, not automatic rejection rules.

More time may be approved where:

- the underlying idea remains strong;
- the problem can be corrected;
- substantial original work remains reusable;
- reworking is cheaper than replacing the concept;
- the revised story would materially strengthen the platform portfolio.

The purpose is to avoid accidental over-investment, not to discourage creative development.

## 4. Stage 0 — Story idea intake

### Objective

Capture enough information to screen the story without developing it substantially.

### Normal effort

Approximately 15–30 minutes.

### Required information

- proposed working title;
- one or more alternative titles where available;
- one-sentence hook;
- short premise of approximately 100–250 words;
- intended genre and audience;
- proposed format and approximate episode count;
- principal characters;
- central story mechanism;
- known inspirations or comparable works;
- whether the concept adapts or deliberately references an existing work;
- creator and proposed IP owner.

### Do not yet create

- ten complete episode summaries;
- a full 100-episode roadmap;
- finished scripts;
- high-resolution production artwork;
- paid voice recordings, music or audio production;
- public promotional campaigns.

### Output

**Story IP Intake Record**

### Gate 0 decision

- `screen`
- `return_for_information`
- `stop_known_unavailable_material`

Normal original concepts proceed to Stage 1.

## 5. Stage 1 — Rapid title and market screen

### Objective

Identify obvious naming conflicts before meaningful story development begins.

### Normal effort

Approximately 30 minutes.

### Search areas

Search the proposed title and sensible variations across:

- general web search;
- audiobook and podcast platforms;
- book and ebook platforms;
- film and television databases;
- game platforms;
- music platforms;
- social-media names;
- relevant domain names;
- IP Australia TM Checker;
- Australian Trade Mark Search.

Search variations should include:

- the exact title;
- the title without leading words such as “The”;
- singular and plural versions;
- spelling variations;
- similar-sounding expressions;
- the distinctive words individually;
- close translations where commercially relevant.

An ordinary internet search is not formal trade mark clearance. Similar marks, related goods and services, pending applications and significant unregistered commercial uses must also be considered.

### Risk assessment

#### Green

- no materially similar title found in closely related entertainment use;
- only unrelated or minor uses found;
- the title is reasonably distinctive;
- no famous character, franchise or brand association is apparent.

#### Amber

- an identical or similar title exists in another market;
- the title has been used by a small, inactive or unrelated creative project;
- similar trade marks exist but the relevant goods or services may differ;
- the title may be usable but difficult to register;
- more investigation is required.

#### Red

- an identical or deceptively similar trade mark appears to cover related entertainment goods or services;
- the title is strongly identified with an existing story, franchise or entertainment property;
- the proposed presentation could imply affiliation or endorsement;
- use depends on copying recognisable branding, characters or protected expression.

### Output

**Rapid Title Screening Report**

### Gate 1 decision

- `proceed_with_title`
- `proceed_under_working_title`
- `develop_alternative_titles`
- `detailed_clearance_required`
- `do_not_use_title`

`do_not_use_title` rejects the title, not automatically the underlying story.

## 6. Stage 2 — Originality and differentiation review

### Objective

Determine whether the concept has enough original expression and commercial identity to justify further development.

### Normal effort

Approximately 60–90 minutes.

### Review questions

1. What is the central idea?
2. Which elements are ordinary genre conventions?
3. Which elements are specifically original to this story?
4. Are the principal characters recognisably distinct?
5. Is the story world sufficiently differentiated?
6. Is the central mechanism too close to one particular existing work?
7. Does the hook identify this story specifically, or could it describe many works?
8. Can concerning similarities be removed without destroying the central appeal?
9. Are names, locations, organisations or symbols derived from real or fictional sources?
10. Has any text, image, audio or other material been copied into the project?

Similarity is not automatically disqualifying. Stories may share genres, themes, archetypes, historical settings, dramatic structures and general ideas. The concern is whether the project relies on another creator’s specific expression, protected elements or commercial identity.

### Differentiation methods

A concept may be reworked by changing:

- title;
- protagonist;
- character relationships;
- setting;
- central object or mechanism;
- source of conflict;
- narrative perspective;
- tone;
- rules of the story world;
- visual identity;
- ending or long-term direction.

### Output

**Story Differentiation Statement**, containing:

- central premise;
- known comparable works;
- common genre elements;
- distinctive story elements;
- identified risks;
- required changes;
- reusable elements;
- recommended decision.

### Gate 2 decision

- `original_enough`
- `minor_differentiation_required`
- `rework_and_resubmit`
- `retain_premise_rebuild_execution`
- `hold`
- `abandon`

## 7. Rework loop

The Rework Loop prevents a strong concept from being discarded merely because its first title or execution creates risk.

### Normal rework allowance

- one title-only rework;
- one concept-differentiation rework;
- one final reassessment.

Additional work may be approved when the story remains commercially or creatively promising.

### Rework record

After each rework, record:

- the original concern;
- what changed;
- what was deliberately retained;
- whether the change resolved the concern;
- whether the story remains worthwhile;
- whether further work is proportionate.

### Reusable Concept Bank

When a story cannot proceed, independently created or unprotected elements may be stored separately, including:

- broad themes;
- genre combinations;
- original character dynamics;
- narrative structures;
- world-building rules;
- episode mechanisms;
- emotional arcs.

Copied or potentially protected third-party expression must not be stored as reusable platform material.

## 8. Stage 3 — Limited concept development

### Objective

Create enough material to judge story quality before formal clearance or substantial production.

### Normal effort

No more than approximately three hours or one working session unless specifically approved.

### Permitted development

- final candidate title and two reserve titles;
- refined hook;
- one-page story summary;
- principal character summaries;
- story-world summary;
- proposed first-season arc;
- summaries for up to three representative episodes;
- broad 100-episode direction expressed as phases or arcs only;
- temporary mood references;
- low-cost internal concept images where necessary.

### Not yet permitted

- complete Episodes 1–10;
- a detailed 100-episode roadmap;
- high-resolution production artwork;
- commissioned voice performances;
- paid audio generation at scale;
- public promotion;
- merchandise;
- substantial story-specific website development.

### Output

**Limited Story Concept Pack**

### Gate 3 tests

#### Creative test

- worthwhile premise;
- sustainable episode engine;
- distinct characters and world;
- plausible long-term direction.

#### IP test

- acceptable title risk;
- acceptable originality risk;
- identifiable ownership position;
- no unresolved red findings.

### Gate 3 decision

- `approve_for_clearance`
- `approve_under_working_title`
- `return_to_rework`
- `hold`
- `close_retain_reusable_elements`

## 9. Stage 4 — Formal pre-draft clearance

### Objective

Complete the final internal IP review before the full Audio Platform Draft Pipeline begins.

### 9.1 Title clearance

- repeat Australian Trade Mark Search;
- review active and pending marks;
- consider similar marks, not merely exact matches;
- compare relevant goods and services;
- review prominent unregistered commercial uses;
- confirm domain and social-name strategy;
- determine whether professional advice is proportionate.

### 9.2 Source-material clearance

Classify every material influence as:

- `original`
- `general_inspiration`
- `public_domain`
- `licensed`
- `commissioned`
- `uncertain`
- `prohibited`

For claimed public-domain material, record the source, author, publication date, jurisdiction considered, version used and whether a modern translation, adaptation, illustration or recording remains protected.

### 9.3 Ownership clearance

Confirm ownership of:

- story summaries;
- episode scripts;
- artwork and character designs;
- maps and diagrams;
- music and sound effects;
- narration;
- software-generated assets;
- contractor-created materials.

Contractor and contributor agreements must state who owns or may use the resulting IP. Do not assume payment alone transfers all rights.

### 9.4 Licence register

For every external asset, retain:

- source;
- creator;
- licence;
- acquisition date;
- permitted uses;
- attribution requirements;
- modification rights;
- commercial-use rights;
- evidence of the licence terms.

### 9.5 AI-production record

Record:

- tool used;
- generation date;
- prompt or production brief;
- source images supplied;
- whether third-party characters, brands or styles were requested;
- human editing, selection or revision undertaken;
- intended asset owner;
- relevant service terms retained at the time of creation.

### Output

**Pre-Draft IP Clearance Record**

### Gate 4 decision

- `cleared`
- `cleared_with_conditions`
- `cleared_working_title`
- `professional_review_required`
- `return_to_rework`
- `rejected`

Only `cleared`, `cleared_with_conditions` and `cleared_working_title` stories may enter the Audio Platform Draft Pipeline.

## 10. Stage 5 — Draft Pipeline handover

The IP Pipeline creates the authorised input to the Audio Platform Draft Pipeline.

### Required handover fields

- story ID or temporary intake ID;
- approved working title;
- approved public title where determined;
- alternative titles;
- approved hook and premise;
- approved character names;
- approved world terminology;
- prohibited or reserved elements;
- source-material restrictions;
- ownership status;
- trade mark risk;
- clearance conditions;
- IP approval date;
- reviewer;
- next review date.

### Draft controls

During drafting:

- new major characters must not deliberately reproduce existing characters;
- newly introduced organisations, magical systems or branded objects must be checked where they become commercially important;
- references to real people or businesses must be flagged;
- adaptations and quotations must be recorded;
- significant changes to the title, hook or core premise return the story to this pipeline.

## 11. Stage 6 — Trade mark investment gate

### Objective

Determine whether the title is commercially important enough to justify a trade mark application.

Not every experimental story should immediately incur trade mark costs.

### Indicators supporting an application

- the story has passed Draft or Review;
- it is intended to become an ongoing series;
- the title is distinctive;
- the title will be used prominently as a commercial brand;
- substantial artwork, audio or promotion is planned;
- public release is approaching;
- internal quality assessment supports continued production;
- audience testing supports the story;
- licensing, publishing or merchandise may be pursued;
- losing the title would create meaningful rework or commercial loss.

### Normally defer where

- the concept remains experimental;
- the title is temporary;
- the story may be abandoned after initial review;
- no public release is planned;
- the title is descriptive or commercially weak;
- ownership has not been settled;
- intended goods, services and markets remain unclear.

### Application preparation

1. Repeat free title and trade mark searches.
2. Determine the correct legal owner of the mark.
3. Identify the genuine intended goods and services.
4. Check current IP Australia classes, descriptions, fees and application processes.
5. Choose between a standard application, TM Headstart or professional clearance.
6. Record the commercial approval and budget.

For an audio-fiction series, potentially relevant areas may include:

- Class 9 for downloadable digital or audio content;
- Class 41 for entertainment, publishing and non-downloadable online content;
- Class 16 for printed publications where genuinely intended.

These are planning indicators only. The correct specification must be checked against the current IP Australia classification and the actual intended use before filing.

### Gate 6 decision

- `headstart`
- `standard_application`
- `professional_clearance`
- `use_and_monitor`
- `delay_pending_review`
- `select_stronger_mark`
- `do_not_pursue_registration`

## 12. Stage 7 — Review Pipeline IP check

When a story enters the Audio Platform Review Pipeline, confirm:

- the final story still matches the cleared premise;
- the final title matches the approved title;
- no later episode introduced problematic third-party material;
- all artwork has provenance and ownership records;
- music and sound-effect licences permit intended use;
- narrator and contractor rights are documented;
- trade mark status is current;
- all clearance conditions have been met.

### Output

**Review-Stage IP Confirmation**

### Decision

- `confirmed`
- `confirmed_with_corrections`
- `return_to_ip_pipeline`
- `do_not_publish`

## 13. Stage 8 — Public release check

Immediately before public release:

- repeat a current title search;
- confirm no new conflicting application or prominent use has appeared;
- confirm ownership and attribution notices;
- confirm final licences;
- confirm trade mark application or registration status;
- use ™ only as an unregistered trade mark claim where appropriate;
- use ® only after registration permits it;
- archive the published version and its production evidence.

### Output

**Public IP Release Approval**

## 14. Pipeline risk classifications

### Green — acceptable

- no meaningful title conflict identified;
- original expression is evident;
- ownership is documented;
- licences are valid;
- no professional escalation is required.

### Amber — manageable

- similar uses exist;
- the title may be usable but difficult to protect;
- differentiation is required;
- ownership or licence evidence needs completion;
- the story may proceed under a working title;
- professional advice may be appropriate before public launch.

### Red — unresolved and unacceptable

- probable conflict with a relevant registered or pending mark;
- recognisable copying of protected expression;
- unlicensed adaptation;
- uncertain ownership of central assets;
- reliance on protected characters, worlds, branding or recordings;
- public use likely to imply unauthorised association.

Red means stop or replace the affected element. It does not automatically mean abandon the entire project.

## 15. Time and expenditure controls

| Stage | Maximum normal commitment |
|---|---:|
| Idea intake | 15–30 minutes |
| Rapid title screen | Approximately 30 minutes |
| Differentiation review | 60–90 minutes |
| Limited concept pack | Up to three hours |
| Formal clearance | Proportionate to risk |
| Full draft production | Only after clearance |
| High-resolution artwork | Only after Draft approval |
| Production audio | Only after Review approval or a defined production gate |
| Trade mark expenditure | Only after commercial approval |

These controls are not automatic rejection deadlines. Any extension must state why additional work remains worthwhile.

## 18. Non-negotiable rules

1. No full Episodes 1–10 production before IP clearance.
2. No production audio before IP clearance and narrative approval.
3. No high-resolution final artwork before title and concept clearance.
4. No public launch while ownership of a central asset remains unresolved.
5. A title conflict must be assessed separately from the underlying story.
6. Amber findings must produce specific conditions, not vague warnings.
7. Every rejection must state which original elements remain reusable.
8. Every external asset must have a known source and licence.
9. Contractor and contributor agreements must address IP ownership and permitted use.
10. Significant title, hook or premise changes trigger reassessment.
11. Trade mark registration is a commercial decision, not a requirement for testing every story.
12. An ordinary web search is not formal trade mark clearance.
13. Professional advice is required where the risk and planned investment justify it.

## 20. Reusable instruction

Use this instruction before beginning substantial development of a new story:

> **Audio Platform IP and Trade Mark Pipeline**
>
> Screen “[TITLE]” before full development. Review the title and sensible variations, comparable entertainment uses, trade mark risk, originality, ownership and licences. Follow `documentation/pipelines/audio-platform-ip-trademark-pipeline.md`.
>
> Spend only enough time to complete the current gate. Treat title risk separately from story value. Where the idea remains worthwhile, prefer renaming, differentiation or controlled reworking over abandonment. Do not begin the full Audio Platform Draft Pipeline until the story is cleared, conditionally cleared or approved under a working title.
